## 1. What Omega Does

Omega is a discovery and installation interface for third-party plugins.

Omega searches, indexes, and displays plugins and plugin repositories that are publicly discoverable and can be processed by Omega's catalogue systems.

The catalogue is continuously updated as sources become available, change, disappear, or are discovered.

As a result, Omega may display plugins from:

- official or commonly used plugin repositories;
- independent third-party repositories;
- individual plugin developers;
- community-maintained repositories;
- repositories that have not been reviewed or approved by the developers of Omega, Dalamud, XIVLauncher, Square Enix, or any other party.

**A plugin appearing in Omega does not mean that Omega recommends, approves, audits, certifies, or guarantees that plugin.**

Omega makes plugins easier to discover and install. It does not make third-party software inherently safe.

## 2. Third-Party Plugins Are Executable Software

Plugins are software.

Installing a plugin is therefore fundamentally different from viewing a website, image, or other passive content.

A plugin may execute programming code on your computer and may operate inside or alongside FINAL FANTASY XIV and Dalamud.

Depending on how a plugin has been written, it may be technically capable of accessing resources available to the game process or to your operating-system user account.

This may include, but is not limited to:

- information stored in or accessible through the FINAL FANTASY XIV game client;
- game memory and internal game data;
- character information;
- account-related identifiers known to the game client;
- player, party, Free Company, social, inventory, combat, world and other game information;
- chat messages or other information visible to the game client;
- network communications;
- files and directories your Windows user account is permitted to access;
- configuration files;
- other running processes or software interfaces;
- input devices and input events;
- clipboard contents;
- locally stored information; and
- information that a plugin deliberately sends to an external server or service.

The exact access and behaviour of a plugin depends entirely on its implementation.

**Omega cannot guarantee that a third-party plugin will only access the information necessary for its advertised function.**

A malicious, compromised, abandoned, vulnerable, or poorly designed plugin could potentially expose information, damage or alter local data, interfere with other software, compromise your privacy, or otherwise harm your computer or accounts.

## 3. Plugin Security

You should treat the installation of a plugin in the same way you would treat the installation of other executable software from the Internet.

A plugin may change after an update.

A repository may change ownership.

A developer account or distribution server may be compromised.

A previously trustworthy plugin may therefore become unsafe in the future.

Security software, source-code availability, popularity, community reputation, previous safe use, or inclusion in a known repository can reduce uncertainty, but none of these things guarantees that a plugin is safe.

**You are responsible for deciding whether you trust a plugin and its developer before installing or using it.**

Consequences may include restrictions, temporary suspension, permanent account termination, or other action by Square Enix.

Omega cannot determine whether a particular plugin, feature, configuration, or method of use will result in enforcement against your account.

The absence of previous enforcement does not mean that a plugin or activity is permitted or safe.

**You use third-party plugins at your own account risk.**

## 5. Automation Carries Increased Risk

You should exercise particular caution with plugins that automate actions.

Automation may include plugins that automatically:

- move your character;
- navigate through the game world;
- enter or complete duties;
- participate in combat;
- select abilities;
- gather resources;
- craft items;
- interact with NPCs;
- purchase or sell items;
- interact with the market;
- manage inventory;
- send messages;
- perform repetitive actions;
- continue playing without active user input; or
- otherwise perform actions that would normally be performed manually by a player.

Automation can make behaviour easier to identify and may directly conflict with game rules concerning bots, automation software, unattended play, or other prohibited third-party functionality.

**Using a plugin to automate gameplay may substantially increase the risk of action being taken against your FINAL FANTASY XIV account, including permanent loss of the account.**

This remains your responsibility even when the plugin was discovered or installed through Omega.

## 6. Review a Plugin Before Installing It

Omega provides plugin overview pages to help you make an informed decision.

Before installing an unfamiliar plugin, you are encouraged to open its **Plugin Overview** and browse the information and pages provided there.

Depending on the information published by the plugin or repository, these pages may provide access to:

- the plugin description;
- the developer or author;
- the source repository;
- the plugin's website;
- documentation;
- release information;
- changelogs;
- issue trackers;
- source information;
- repository information; and
- other links supplied by the plugin developer.

Use these links to investigate the developer and plugin yourself.

For example, you may review:

- who maintains the plugin;
- whether source code is available;
- whether development is active;
- the plugin's recent changes;
- reported bugs or security concerns;
- its documentation;
- the repository from which it will be installed; and
- whether you recognise and trust the developer.

When a plugin is available from multiple repositories, Omega may ask you to choose which repository you want to use.

**Do not install a plugin if you do not trust its developer, repository, or origin.**
